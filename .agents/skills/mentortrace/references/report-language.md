# Author-facing bilingual report rules

For source quotations, follow source-fidelity.md. Original must reproduce the verified manuscript expression, including decisive delimiters, operators and indices; reuse a checked transcription when supplied. Do not copy corrupted extracted math into the report. Inspect the rendered formula against the original page. Correcting a source quotation also requires checking its comment, suggested revisions and shared actions; a contradicted premise cannot survive as a confirmed defect merely because the quote was repaired.

Apply these rules after the findings and their source dispositions are frozen. They govern the author-facing Markdown and any HTML/PDF export; they do not alter evidence judgments, finding counts, or advisor-comparison policy.

Do not include a “Reading order / 阅读方式” paragraph explaining grouping, numbering, section sequence or navigation in the author-facing usage notes. Apply those organization rules directly to the report.

Use a prominent “MentorTrace” report title, such as “MentorTrace — Manuscript Review and Revision Report / 论文审阅与修改报告”. Place both English `Manuscript` and Chinese `稿件` metadata directly below the title and above “How to use this report / 使用说明”. In 使用说明, briefly identify MentorTrace Skill and the actual recorded review model/effort. Do not assume every future run uses gpt-6-sol / xhigh. Do not narrate report-version history or say that the current version has adjusted structure or expression. Keep usage notes concise; do not add paragraphs explaining locator mechanics, field layout, figure-float conventions, cross-section navigation, or group/draft mechanics that are already clear from the report itself. This basic generation note is user-facing; detailed lane, batch, reuse and merge provenance remains private. Include a concise bilingual use-and-verification note: AI-assisted review may contain errors or omissions and does not replace the author's judgment; an unclear comment can be explained by an AI tool using the relevant manuscript passage, without inventing missing facts; authors must check both the report and any AI explanation against the manuscript, derivations and experimental records before adopting changes. This note does not excuse unclear report writing or substitute for making each comment self-contained.

1. **Within each numbered problem or coherent report block, present the English account together before the Chinese account.** Bilingual title and section headings may remain paired. In the opening usage section, put all English paragraphs together first, then all Chinese paragraphs in the same topic order; the two priority explanations remain separate, unbolded paragraphs in their respective blocks. For content and organization problems, put English priority (if applicable), location, manuscript excerpt, comment and action together before the Chinese review and revision fields. Give the source passage once under `**Original:**`; do not repeat priority, location or manuscript excerpts in the Chinese part of a numbered comment. For coordinated plans and use notes, complete the English explanation before the Chinese explanation. Language comments place Location → Original → Comment → Suggested revision in English, then 审阅意见 → 建议修改 in Chinese; the replacement text itself is the same English manuscript wording in both blocks, without a Chinese translation. Each language's explanatory prose should read naturally on its own. Translate the checked meaning, evidence strength, conditions, and requested revision, not the syntax of the other language.
2. **Edit for a researcher reading the report.** Lead with the specific manuscript question or defect, explain why it affects interpretation, validity or reproducibility, and give the concrete action that would answer it. State what the existing evidence supports when that distinction matters. Do not open with vague “conditional support” or defer the issue to another comment's “scope limit”. Replace internal shorthand such as “information prerequisite,” “relation gap,” or “scope the claim” with the actual information, connection, or wording needed. A direct question can help when the author must supply an unknown fact, but do not turn every comment into a question or impose a fixed sentence count. Use natural sentences, retain consequential uncertainty and conditions, and avoid defensive caveats that do not change the judgment. More forceful wording is useful only when it states the supported problem more accurately.
3. **Use separate visible numbering for content, organization, and language comments.** Number each type from 1. Group closely related content and technical problems by revision object when they share conditions, passages, or a coordinated repair. Order groups broadly by the manuscript passage where the author must first verify or change an underlying fact, keeping dependent conditions and conclusions together; keep each distinct problem's own number, location, priority, and explanation. Within ungrouped sections, follow manuscript reading order. For a multi-location problem, put that primary verification/edit location first in Location and Original, place the numbered comment with that part of the manuscript, then list earlier definitions and later claims that must be synchronized. For example, an ambiguity exposed by a Section IV simulation parameter belongs with the Section IV parameter comments even when Eq. (2) also needs a changed definition; do not sort it by Eq. (2) merely because that is the first formula it affects. Abstract/contribution/conclusion claim problems may remain at their claim passages, but their revision instructions should identify the later evidence that must be checked first. Recheck every section's numbering, group heading and cross-reference after moving a comment. Do not expose `verify:Fxx`, `language:Uxx`, lane-internal labels, or database keys. Preserve private mappings across regrouping. Numbered cross-references may aid navigation but must never carry information needed to understand the problem or action.
4. **Use the author-facing section order “How to use this report / 使用说明,” “Content and technical comments / 内容与技术意见,” “Organization and flow comments / 篇章与行文意见,” “Language and presentation comments / 语言与格式意见,” and finally “Coverage and remaining checks / 覆盖范围与待核对事项.”** Describe comment types in that same order. Do not prefix organization titles, category descriptions, counts, or cross-references with “optional / 可选”; cite an item as “organization and flow comment 7 / 篇章与行文意见 7.” Omit the closing comment-type sentence about separate numbering and optional repairs. Preserve the actual strength and conditions of each recommendation without changing its evidence judgment. Place revision-priority meaning and wording-use conditions together in 使用说明. Except for the brief actual model/effort generation note, keep detailed operational provenance (lanes, batches, source replay, raw responses and merge records) in a separate private record, not the author report. Do not use “surface” in author-facing language. Internal `finding_type=surface` and machine filenames may remain unchanged.
5. **Use explicit fields, with each field in its own Markdown paragraph.** For each content or organization comment, show `Revision priority` where applicable, then `Location`, `Original`, `Comment`, and any `Suggested revision` in English, followed by `审阅意见` and any `建议修改` in Chinese. Omit `修改优先级`, `位置`, and `原文` fields from every numbered Chinese block. The Original field reproduces substantial, faithful, searchable manuscript sentence(s); include enough context to distinguish the passage, and never invent a quotation. For content and organization problems, favor a complete distinctive source sentence plus the neighboring sentence that establishes the questioned link when a short phrase cannot locate the passage. A figure legend or brief caption can remain short when paired with its complete explanatory sentence and figure/formula anchor. Avoid excerpts so long that the disputed sentence becomes hard to spot. Put the primary verification/edit passage first in the Original field, followed by the earlier or later passages that must be synchronized. When there are multiple excerpts, put each on its own Markdown paragraph or hard-break line under the single Original label. Move source tags such as `(Introduction)` from after a quotation to immediately before that quotation in the English Original field. Give the page, left/right column where verifiable, section or figure, and equation number where applicable. For a formula-centered issue, include the relevant formula or disputed expression as well as its neighboring prose in the Original field; transcribe it from the page image and identify its number. Typeset an original display equation as a separate display-math block and an original inline expression as inline math. Preserve mathematical notation and the equation's display/inline role; do not flatten a formula into prose or silently turn a prose expression into a displayed source equation. Normal PDF line-wrap repair is allowed. For ungrouped content and organization items, place `Suggested revision` inside the English block and `建议修改` inside the Chinese block. For grouped content comments, retain the English priority, location, Original and Comment plus the Chinese 审阅意见 under each numbered problem, then give the group's English revision plan, Chinese revision plan and coordinated drafts immediately after its problems. Organize the plan by concrete actions so every original requirement remains visible; grouping must not collapse independently answerable problems. The comment fields explain the issue; the revision fields specify the proposed changes. State exactly where to add, delete, replace, or move text. For a cross-section issue, state where the author can first establish the missing fact or evaluate the result, then which earlier definition and later summary must be synchronized; do not make the author infer an edit path from page order. Provide usable wording when supported by the manuscript; where facts are missing, identify the information the author must supply and make any placeholder or conditional wording explicit. Do not invent technical facts. If a suggested English sentence, title, caption or paragraph is actionable in the Chinese block, reproduce that exact English wording there rather than referring to “the English sentence above / 上面的英文句子.” Keep each selected action and its conditions explicit. For coordinated replacement passages, use the single-draft presentation rule below rather than duplicating a complete draft in multiple actions. For language comments, use English `Location`, `Original`, `Comment`, `Suggested revision`, then Chinese `审阅意见`, `建议修改`. Put the exact same corrected English wording after both suggested-revision labels; do not translate the replacement sentence into Chinese. If a replacement depends on technical clarification, label it conditional rather than presenting it as a definite edit. Locations should specify page, left/right column where applicable, and paragraph, equation, or other verifiable anchor; never guess a column.
   For `Revision priority`, `Location`, `Original`, `Comment`, `审阅意见`, `Suggested revision`, and `建议修改`, place the text immediately after the label's colon on the same line, separated by one space. A separate field paragraph does not mean a separate label-only line. Show the item location once as `**Location:** English location` in the English block. Keep the unbolded `修改优先级：` explanation in 使用说明, but do not add a Chinese priority field to numbered comments. Keep English and Chinese comment fields in separate paragraphs.
6. **Preserve uncertainty and optional wording separately.** Do not count a possible ambiguity as a proven error, and do not silently turn an optional suggestion into a required correction.
7. **Check both languages before delivery.** Construct the English and Chinese explanations as natural prose for each language, using the checked meaning rather than mirroring sentence structure. Compare each version with its frozen finding for the same technical terms, referents, negations, quantifiers, numerical values, uncertainty, applicability conditions, requested action and optional status. Do not turn “control a term” into “make it vanish,” or “may remain valid” into “is valid.” Then compare the English and Chinese paragraphs, verify displayed numbering and the private ID mapping, and confirm the Markdown renders with the intended line breaks. Generate HTML/PDF only when requested.

## Concise revision requests and language comments

Use Suggested revision / 建议修改 for both supported edits and requests for missing author facts. Include what to supply, where it belongs and which statements depend on it in those fields or the shared revision actions. Do not create a separate Author insert, Author information required — insertion point, Information needed from the author, or equivalent field, heading or standalone checklist. Fold any such content into the revision actions and remove duplicated requests without losing their conditions.

Keep explanations proportional to the issue. The Comment states the problem and its consequence; Suggested revision states what to do. Do not repeat the full diagnosis in the action, the same fact checklist in draft instructions, or the same warning throughout a group. Keep a condition beside any passage whose safe use depends on it, even when it was mentioned earlier. Concision does not remove necessary technical evidence, genuine alternatives or unresolved author decisions.

For a straightforward language correction, retain the existing six fields and bilingual order, but use a short, searchable Original span and an exact replacement span rather than copying a long sentence or paragraph unnecessarily. Usually a brief grammatical reason in Comment and 审阅意见 is sufficient; avoid grammar lessons, speculative consequences, repeated scope disclaimers and unrelated technical discussion. Include more context when needed to preserve referents, meaning or a conditional correction. Preserve every verified occurrence anchor and correction; do not drop small errors to shorten the report. Keep the same corrected English text in both revision fields.

Reconcile overlapping local and full-passage drafts using repair-contract.md. Print the selected complete wording once in its shared draft block; a local action can identify the exact change incorporated there without reproducing the full passage. This exception does not permit vague actions or hiding their prerequisites behind a numbered cross-reference. Give multiple complete alternatives only when they resolve genuinely different choices the author must make. Do not reproduce unchanged formulas or neighboring paragraphs merely to make a local correction look complete; include them when necessary for a coherent, usable replacement.

Keep usage notes and closing coverage notes short. Report actual unreviewed areas, unresolved decisions and material verification limits; do not repeat a section-by-section inventory of the delivered recommendations or narrate internal execution. Preserve concrete unresolved questions in their appropriate report location and avoid a second full copy in the closing notes. Page count and comment count are not compression targets.

## Concrete explanation examples

These generic contrasts illustrate how to make a checked finding actionable. They do not prescribe new checks, evidence judgments or a universal sentence pattern.

- Vague: “The evaluation requires clarification.” Concrete: “Which observations are included in the reported average? Without the inclusion rule, readers cannot reconstruct the comparison. State the rule and apply it consistently to the reported averages.”
- Vague: “The claim is only conditionally supported.” Concrete: “The experiment tests one operating condition, while the conclusion refers to all operating conditions. Limit the conclusion to the tested condition or provide evidence for the wider claim.”
- Vague: “Improve the organization.” Concrete: “The comparison settings are split between two paragraphs. Collecting the stated settings before the comparison would help readers match methods to inputs. This rearrangement is optional when the existing account already makes that connection clear.”

Keep the explanation and action self-contained even when a numbered cross-reference is useful. For a proposed language substitution, check that its technical meaning matches the surviving content requirements; label a replacement conditional when author clarification is needed. For replacement drafts and their use instructions, follow the final factual-state check in repair-contract.md. A clearer sentence must not turn proposed work into a completed author action or broaden a result beyond its evidence.

## Report layout and revision blocks

Use unnumbered third-level headings for content revision groups and numbered fourth-level headings for their individual problems. Follow each group with an explicitly scoped heading, for example “Shared revision actions for comments 17–18 / 第 17—18 条的统一修改动作”, concrete bilingual action paragraphs, and any local drafts. Name the actual members (a range only if contiguous); do not rely on “this group” or visual proximity. Do not number group plans or drafts as additional concerns.

Write the scoped shared-plan label as a real fourth-level Markdown heading, never merely a bold paragraph. It follows all numbered comments in its group and belongs to the group, outside every individual comment card. Keep blank paragraphs between explanations and revision fields; HTML/PDF must visibly separate the bilingual explanation from the bilingual action while retaining same-line field labels. The exact print spacing and group containers are defined in report-pdf.md and the CSS.

For every local or coordinated draft, name its replacement location in the heading or Use field. Add a concise English Scope and Chinese 对应修改 paragraph explaining which concrete requirements it implements, which repair alternative it takes, and what remains to be done elsewhere. Numbers may identify ownership but cannot replace that explanation. The shared revision actions are the complete checklist for all named problems. A draft immediately following them is an example for a named passage, not a second finding or a substitute for the checklist; state plainly which actions it implements and which author-supplied assumptions, calculations or experiments remain. A draft following a shared plan may implement only part of it; never imply that pasting the draft supplies missing assumptions, evidence or experiments. For abstracts, contributions and conclusions that coordinate several comments, state that broader scope rather than implying ownership by the nearest comment. If both a local sentence and a full paragraph replacement target the same passage, identify them as alternatives, not cumulative insertions. Preserve all evidence judgments and conditions when clarifying these relationships.

Earlier reports may illustrate layout, but their issue counts, group counts, page counts and technical conclusions are never template constants for a new manuscript.

When replacing an earlier report on the same manuscript, keep independent new discovery blind to that report. After its raw findings are frozen, compare earlier author-facing items one by one with the new verified outputs. For each absent item, record whether it is covered by a new item, remains supported and needs delivery, is contradicted by anchored manuscript evidence, or remains uncertain. Do not treat a smaller Language output as successful deduplication without this accounting. Preserve the prior report and the comparison record.

Explain revision priority inside 使用说明 after the English comment-type paragraph as `Revision priority: English explanation`, and in the corresponding position of the later Chinese block as `修改优先级： 中文说明`. Keep these two labels unbolded and on the same line as their explanation; do not join them to the comment-type paragraph. “High / 高” means the issue should be addressed first because it affects the main argument, understanding of the method, or reproducibility; “Medium / 中” concerns a local explanation, definition, or scope. Priority describes the importance of revision, not confidence that an error exists. Keep the condition that suggested wording is usable only when its stated premises hold in these paragraphs. Keep confirmed issues, items needing confirmation, and optional suggestions distinct. Ordinary language corrections do not require a priority label.

Use “Organization and flow comments / 篇章与行文意见” as a distinct section before language comments. Attach coordinated rewrites and missing-fact checklists directly to the relevant numbered item; do not collect them in a detached suggested-wording section. For each comment, show the current location/order, proposed destination/order, reader benefit, affected transitions/references and any conditions, in English then Chinese. Necessary technical or explanatory repairs remain in content and technical comments with their concrete details and local wording. Internally optional suggestions are not added to required content-problem counts. Use the organization disposition record to check that every proposed rearrangement was delivered, incorporated elsewhere, or excluded with a reason.

Use the same explicit English `Location`, `Original`, `Comment`, `Suggested revision` and Chinese `审阅意见`, `建议修改` fields for organization items. Put current order and reader benefit in the comment; put the proposed order, affected references and conditions in the suggested revision. Do not revert to `English` or `中文` field labels in organization items, draft conditions, coverage or limitations. General bilingual notes can use an English paragraph followed by a Chinese paragraph, except for opening usage notes, which group all English paragraphs before all Chinese paragraphs. These are presentation rules, not new findings. Do not flag a figure solely because normal multi-column floating layout places it at the top of a page before the prose discussion; assess the first textual callout, caption self-sufficiency where needed, and any genuinely missing information separately.

For retained section-level revisions, preserve the overall purpose and anchored retain/delete/compress/move/add/rewrite actions in Suggested revision. Necessary repairs remain content comments; optional selection and restructuring proposals use numbered items in the organization section. When authorized and supported, place coordinated English wording below its main comment or the shared revision plan of its group. Explain which requirements it implements in plain language. Repeat a short prerequisite where necessary instead of making the reader follow a chain of comment numbers. Preserve independently answerable concerns when coordinating their revision. Do not translate English manuscript replacement text merely to fill a bilingual field. State author-dependent conditions beside the draft.

Make replacement passages easy to use. Suggested wording implements already numbered comments; it is not an independently counted concern. Keep that accounting in internal records rather than explaining Skill workflow in author-facing usage notes. If drafting reveals a new issue, return it to review and verification before adding a numbered comment. Use short draft headings, followed by English and Chinese use instructions and the replacement text. Explain the actual dependency before its cross-reference: for example, state which experimental procedure must be confirmed before citing its comment number. Avoid long headings made of readiness labels and lists of comment IDs, or vague directions such as “see the draft below.”

Within each shared revision plan, place all English `Suggested revision` action paragraphs consecutively, followed by all corresponding Chinese `建议修改` paragraphs in the same order. Keep the plan outside the numbered cards. Do not pair the last English paragraph with the first Chinese paragraph, draw per-action boxes, or put a shaded frame around the whole plan. A separator may mark the language change.

Treat every local replacement draft as one independent block, including formula corrections such as a vector equality. Inside that block, use this order: its bilingual heading; English `Use`, `Scope`, and any other English use instruction; a separator; the corresponding Chinese use/scope/action explanation; a second separator; one `**Suggested wording / 建议文本:**` label; and one complete English replacement passage. Keep each field's value on the same line except the wording passage. Do not print the replacement passage before the Chinese explanation or repeat it after the explanation. The Chinese instruction should identify the insertion point and conditions without duplicating the full English passage; the one bilingual label introduces the shared wording. Preserve all substantive conditions when consolidating duplicates, and check that multiple copies are identical before removing one.

Keep each draft with the problem it resolves and name its exact replacement location. Distinguish a supported draft, a draft requiring specified confirmation, and facts the author still needs to supply without making the author decode generic category labels. If one sentence depends on an unverified result, identify that sentence and explain the alternatives. When alternatives materially affect a paragraph, provide complete, clearly conditioned versions rather than asking the author to assemble separated passages. A version that omits an unresolved result is an interim treatment; explain how the version retaining it resolves the original concern. Preserve the original recommended insertion point and surrounding transitions. State missing-fact requests as concrete actions inside Suggested revision / 建议修改 or the shared revision actions, not as an additional field, a new comment, paste-ready prose or a placeholder paragraph.

For a presentation-only revision, preserve the prior report and compare every old comment with its new counterpart for location, evidence, uncertainty, priority, requested action and conditions. Keep an internal mapping for relocated drafts and record any correction of conflicting placement instructions. A structural comparison is useful evidence of retention, but semantic equivalence also requires reading the edited explanations in both languages. Do not claim this editing pass is a new manuscript review.

## Required preservation gate

For manuscript draft passages, put `**Suggested wording / 建议文本:**` on its own line, with the actual replacement passage in the next paragraph. This is an explicit exception to same-line field rendering; `Suggested revision`, `建议修改`, `Original`, locations and comments retain their existing same-line rules.

Audit every Suggested wording passage for its editing value. Remove a standalone sample that merely paraphrases the problem or duplicates a fuller draft for the same location. When the proposed replacement explains a formula-centered point, express the necessary equation in the manuscript's notation and verify the formula against the page; preserve conditions and alternatives. Where implementation facts are unavailable, provide a concrete verification-and-synchronization path instead of fabricating paste-ready wording. Record removed or consolidated drafts in the preservation audit so no underlying requirement disappears.

Before this preservation gate, complete the revision scope and completeness check in repair-contract.md for every retained content/technical and organization comment or coordinated group. Verify that the delivered sentence, paragraph, move/delete instructions or missing-fact request matches the required repair scope. A populated Suggested revision field is not sufficient; keep the scope decision and any reason for omitting supported paragraph wording in a private record.

Follow delivery-preservation.md before delivering Markdown or exporting HTML/PDF. Preserve exact applicability conditions, decisive evidence, action locations, conditional downstream repairs and valid alternatives. Concision and shared revision plans do not authorize removing these obligations. Compare to original verified sources and earlier delivered reports, not only the last rewrite.
