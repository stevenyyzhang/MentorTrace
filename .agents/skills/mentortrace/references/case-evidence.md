# Evidence classes for published-paper cases

A published paper and an analyst's case record are different evidence objects.
Publication does not certify every interpretation written about that paper.

- Bibliographic metadata, sections and page numbers locate a public source.
- Relations, sentence roles and contextual accounts are analyst reconstructions
  of that source. Verify the source itself if a disputed interpretation matters.
- Reader benefits are hypotheses unless supported by a separately documented
  reader evaluation. An intuitive reduction in search effort is not a measured outcome.
- Writing recipes, target applicability and candidate sufficiency are transfer
  hypotheses. They do not establish a defect or uniquely correct organization.

Do not require an alternative for every source case. No alternative is supplied
when its basis is missing. A possible ordering imagined by an analyst is not a
documented writing example. To recognize a documented alternative, identify its
actual source and relevant passage; then check its conditions and dependencies.
To evaluate a new target proposal, use the target manuscript's available facts,
prerequisites and actual reading sequence. A proposal that moves needed
information after its use may be rejected; the rejection does not prove every
aspect of the source arrangement is necessary.

Keep necessary comprehension information distinct from a preferred format.
Nearby prose, an equation, a caption, an explicit forward reference or another
supported arrangement may supply that information. Do not demand the same
sentence, neighboring sentences, a diagram, a table or a fixed section solely
because the source example used one.

The runtime withholds undocumented Reader alternatives and excludes unvalidated
editing recipes from selected-case and drafting inputs. Develop target revisions
from verified findings and available target facts. No case count, source prestige,
field name or frozen hash establishes semantic correctness.
