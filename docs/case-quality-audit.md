# Published-paper case scope and evidence limits

The public library provides 189 Reader cases, 52 Expert cases, 5 companion summaries and 52 source citations. It contains analytical summaries and public bibliographic references, not source PDFs or verbatim article passages.

The 11 Reader categories and 14 Expert categories are curated navigation groups. Category membership is not an exhaustive assessment, a quality ranking or a prevalence estimate. Categories overlap. See `corpus/published-cases-v1/category_evidence.json` for supporting case IDs and grouping boundaries.

Reader relationships describe information and argument tasks. Reader benefits are hypotheses, not measured reader-study outcomes. Expert case roles distinguish source-task examples, source-stated scope boundaries and mixed records. A source's stated assumption or limitation is not automatically a source defect. Analyst critiques and unvalidated editing recipes are excluded from runtime selected-case inputs.

Use each case with its applicability and transfer boundaries. Cases cannot establish that a target manuscript is wrong or that a proposed rewrite is better. Ground target findings in the manuscript's actual evidence and prerequisites. Consult the original article when a detailed source claim requires verification. The library does not independently re-prove published results, reproduce simulations or establish review accuracy on unseen manuscripts.
