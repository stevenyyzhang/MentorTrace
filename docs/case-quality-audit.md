# What the published-paper cases establish

The library supplies examples of tasks completed in selected published wireless-communications papers and analyst groupings of recurring relationships. It does not establish common advantages of every excellent paper, a ranking of writing quality, a uniquely correct sentence order, or experimentally demonstrated reader benefits.

The library contains 189 Reader cases, 52 Technical cases, 5 companion summaries and 52 source citations. Source PDFs, passages and full-page transcriptions are excluded.

## Cross-paper support

The 11 Reader categories have highlighted instances from 2–4 distinct papers each: 36 references identify 34 cases across 19 papers. For example, the obstacle-to-new-task relationship occurs in R01 across P02, P11 and P42; R07 has three cases but only two independent papers. A category is an analyst abstraction of related tasks, not a claim that every case uses the same technical mechanism or that every paper exhibits the feature.

The 14 Technical categories nominate 4–11 papers each from the 52-paper library. Candidate membership is curated navigation, not an exhaustive count of confirmed strengths. Categories overlap; their counts must not be added as independent observations or used as a population prevalence estimate. `corpus/published-cases-v1/category_evidence.json` lists the supporting IDs, source relationships, grouping boundaries and case roles.

## Source examples and analyst judgments

Technical role review identified 20 source-task examples, 27 examples of source-stated scope boundaries and 5 mixed records: F10, F11, F29, F42 and F44. A source that states its assumptions, unproved parts or limits is illustrating boundary-setting; that is not automatically a source defect. The mixed records contain source material and separate analyst critiques. Their critiques are labeled unverified research notes and excluded from runtime review inputs. Mathematical claims were not independently reproved and simulations were not rerun.

Reader's earlier `reader_core`/`mixed` classifications describe the original analytical grouping; they do not by themselves distinguish source strengths from analyst criticism. P04-C1 and P16-C6 contained superseded negative annotations. Their public summaries now preserve the source's actual scope distinctions and place the rejected criticism in a separate research note. P27:C7 retains its supported implementation-comparison example without the earlier unsupported-slogan claim.

## Systemic field problem and corrections

All 189 Reader records previously had an undocumented `plausible_alternative`, and their `actual_reader_benefit` fields lacked measured reader-effect evidence. That is a systemic labeling and evidence problem; it does not mean every source relationship is wrong. Public records remove those 189 alternatives and label benefits as hypotheses. They never require an invented alternative for every case or infer that a different order is reasonable simply because a model can describe it.

Per-case review also applied qualifications to transfer boundaries and editing conditions, including removing same-sentence or adjacency requirements where information accessibility was the real task. It corrected P36-C06's figure/metric mapping, retained the other-variable global-optimum premise in P45-C4, and retained the optimization/inequality-direction distinction in P51:C2. Historical open-question statements are scoped to the source publication rather than asserted as current facts.

Technical `sufficient_alternative` fields no longer certify a revision. Optional `transfer_hypothesis` notes remain conditional, and `assessed_target_sufficiency` is null. Editing recipes and analyst critiques stay outside selected review and drafting inputs. Technical execution inputs supply source-specific descriptions and evidence limits; Reader inputs supply relationships with explicit analyst and reader-effect status. A route asks a `sufficiency_question` about the actual target manuscript.

## Review limits

Review compared all case summaries with available stored source contexts and prior analytical records, and separately checked navigation support. It did not conduct an exhaustive contemporary literature search, independently validate all proofs, measure reader effects or compare review accuracy on unseen manuscripts. An original article remains necessary when a source claim needs detailed verification. Target findings and proposed edits must be justified by the target manuscript's own evidence and prerequisites.
